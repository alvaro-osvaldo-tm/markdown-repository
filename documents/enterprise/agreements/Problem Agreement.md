This happens due the intensive manual work to archive these needs.

## Problem Statement

The problem is increasing costs due the manual work to apply the document's governance and to keep both business and technical documents relevant.

## Problem Definition

The organisation faces the need to keep both business and technical personal updated in the business rules changes, it's vital because a change in business rules affect the human labor with customer, and it must be tailored in the organisation digital information systems by the technical personal.

Failing in keep these two groups knowing the same business rules (or keeping at 'same page'), generates inconsistencies in labor and degrades customer experiencie.

Also increase effort in the information systems audit, as without an updated business rules implementation documentation is not possible to ensure how the rules was effective implemented by the technical team.

Other management effort as to keep the document governance and treat it as an asset became degraded due the intense need to keep the document consistency manually, it's because every change in document require manual revision and trigger a cascate of manual document and information systems revision.

### Problem Diagnosis

The documents needs to be manually governed due a lack of specialised tools, that force the actors to do repetitively manual work that require attention and generate fatigue.

Due the stress in the manual work, and the required time to complete, both business and technical personal dont' keep at same page because is not possible to produce updated documents when the business rules or policies changes.

### Problems Founds

Without an automated document checking and governance, it's need to realize manual work requiring a considerable amount of attention.

The time and effort to generate an updated documentation every time a business rule changes prevents both business and technical personal to be at 'same page' sharing a common understanding of the business.

Alternatives as using documents baselines and scheduling updates after a considerable amount of business rules changes was proved inefficient because not reduced the dispersion between the business and technical personal.
